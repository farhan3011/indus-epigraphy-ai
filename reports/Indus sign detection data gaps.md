# Two gaps close; the benchmark stays empty

As of 1 October 2026, two of the three data gaps blocking Milestone 1 are closed and the third is not. **No public dataset anywhere pairs real Indus seal photographs with per-sign bounding boxes**: the DM-BiCLab repo holds three sample images and no annotation files, tpsatish95 holds only pipeline outputs, and Harappa.com is copyrighted and blocks scripted access, so the detection benchmark has to be hand-annotated. The Mahadevan-to-Parpola concordance already exists as an undocumented field in the `mayig/indus-valley-script-corpus` repo (334 of 396 Parpola signs mapped, 77% of corpus tokens converting unambiguously), though it is one person's unsourced work. The full IM77 corpus in Mahadevan numbering (2,906 texts, 3,573 lines, 13,372 legible sign tokens) became downloadable in September 2026 from two independent GitHub exports of indusscript.in that agree with each other on every text. The weak point is licensing. The corpus exports, the CISI plate scans and the Harappa.com photographs carry no redistribution grant, so a public MIT repo should commit only code, the student's own annotations, and the few openly licensed items, and fetch everything else by script. The practical route is to annotate 100 to 150 photographs whose sign sequences are already known, and to publish the boxes without the images.

## No downloadable set pairs seal photographs with sign boxes

The two published pipelines that looked like benchmark sources both withheld their data. The DM-BiCLab repository for Dixit et al. 2025 contains ten files: code, a licence, a README and **exactly three sample images, with no XML, JSON or YOLO label file anywhere in the tree** ([GitHub tree](https://api.github.com/repos/DM-BiCLab/Deep-Learning-in-Archiving-Indus-Script-and-Motif-Information/git/trees/main?recursive=1)). Its README says "The full data sets may be obtained from authors upon request" ([README](https://github.com/DM-BiCLab/Deep-Learning-in-Archiving-Indus-Script-and-Motif-Information/blob/main/README.md)). The paper describes 963 deduplicated seal images drawn from the CISI volumes and Mahadevan 1977, with box annotation on 232 training and 13 validation images ([JCAA](https://journal.caa-international.org/articles/10.5334/jcaa.175)); those figures were read through an automated page summary and should be re-checked against the PDF before being quoted. Even if the authors share the set, it is limited. The sign classifier covers only 40 classes labelled with Mahadevan numbers ([MA.py](https://github.com/DM-BiCLab/Deep-Learning-in-Archiving-Indus-Script-and-Motif-Information/blob/main/Training%20and%20Validation%20Phase/MA.py)), and the detector appears to be a single-class "grapheme" box, although that was not confirmed from the paper text.

The tpsatish95 repository (Palaniappan and Adhikari 2017) is larger but no more useful. It holds code, trained Caffe weights and pipeline output images for about 64 seals, and **no box coordinates in any format** ([GitHub tree](https://api.github.com/repos/tpsatish95/OCR-on-Indus-Seals/git/trees/master?recursive=1)). The 1,150-image "Indus Seals Dataset" the paper describes is absent; about 800 of those images were scans supplied by the Roja Muthiah Research Library ([paper draft](https://github.com/tpsatish95/OCR-on-Indus-Seals/blob/master/paper/paper_draft.md)). The method never used sign-level ground truth. It segments with selective search and a text/no-text classifier, and its only "symbol" label is a per-image flag for whether a jar sign is present. No sign-numbering scheme appears in the released material.

Harappa.com has good photographs but returned a Cloudflare block page to scripted requests on the check date ([Harappa seals listing](https://www.harappa.com/category/slide-subject/seals)). The closest thing to a ready image set is `akksshhaay/Indus-Seal-Dataset`, which contains 139 seal photographs taken from Harappa.com pages, 97 of them with a pre-cropped inscription strip, no boxes, no sign IDs and no licence file ([repo](https://github.com/akksshhaay/Indus-Seal-Dataset)). Searches of Hugging Face, Zenodo, Kaggle and Roboflow Universe found sign drawings and text corpora but no annotated seal photographs. The Kaggle and Roboflow checks went through web search only, and Mendeley Data was not searched, so a small unindexed upload remains possible. A Springer chapter on allograph identification ([link](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-06744-9_19)) sat behind a login and its data statement is unread.

Unannotated photographs are plentiful. The largest free pool is the CISI volume scans on the Internet Archive, where each photograph is printed with its CISI identifier; the item named `corpus-vol-2` is in fact Volume 1 ([vol 1](https://archive.org/details/corpus-vol-2), [vol 2](https://archive.org/details/corpus-vol-2_202409), [bundle with Mahadevan 1977](https://archive.org/details/TheIndusScript.TextConcordanceAndTablesIravathanMahadevan)). The `dixitsandeep/indus_seal_extraction` repo already has scripts that cut individual seal photographs out of those PDF pages and OCR their catalogue IDs; its `.box` files are character boxes for the printed captions, not for Indus signs ([repo](https://github.com/dixitsandeep/indus_seal_extraction)). Wikimedia Commons yields about 135 files across the relevant categories, some of them drawings or display shots ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Indus_civilization_seals)). Open museum images are few: four Met objects confirmed as Indus and public domain ([Met API](https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/public/collection/v1/objects/324062)) and three at Cleveland ([CMA API](https://openaccess-api.clevelandart.org/api/artworks/?q=Indus%20seal&has_image=1&limit=20)). Met keyword searches return 42 or 68 hits depending on the query, but most are not Indus and each must be filtered on the `culture` field.

## The concordance was already in the student's repo, unadvertised

Every `features/Pnnn.json` file in `mayig/indus-valley-script-corpus` carries a `mahadevan_graphemes` array alongside Parpola and Wells lists, so the repo is itself a Parpola-to-Mahadevan-to-Wells lookup table ([P086.json](https://github.com/mayig/indus-valley-script-corpus/blob/main/features/P086.json)). The code documents the field as "a correspondence list of graphemes from Mahadevan's 1977 sign list matching this Parpola sign" ([lib.rs](https://github.com/mayig/indus-valley-script-corpus/blob/main/indus_corpus/src/lib.rs)); the README mentions only the Wells mapping and gives no source or method for the Mahadevan column ([README](https://github.com/mayig/indus-valley-script-corpus/blob/main/README.md)). The Parpola numbering is the 1982 list, not the 1994 one.

Counts made over a clone of the repo at its single commit give the shape of the mapping ([features/](https://github.com/mayig/indus-valley-script-corpus/tree/main/features), [corpus/](https://github.com/mayig/indus-valley-script-corpus/tree/main/corpus)). These are the researchers' own computations, not published figures.

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Parpola IDs with at least one Mahadevan equivalent | 334 of 396 |
| Parpola signs mapping to exactly one Mahadevan sign | 305 |
| Parpola signs mapping to 2, 3 or 5 Mahadevan signs | 24, 3, 2 |
| Mahadevan numbers reachable | 359 of 417 |
| Mahadevan numbers listed under two Parpola signs | 13 |
| Corpus tokens converting unambiguously | 768 of 1,003 (77%) |
| Corpus tokens hitting a one-to-many case | 202 (20%) |
| Corpus tokens with no Mahadevan equivalent | 33 (3%) |

Parpola merges more allographs than Mahadevan, so Parpola-to-Mahadevan is the lossy direction and the reverse is nearly a function. P086 (tree) covers five Mahadevan signs and P122 covers M099 and M100 ([P122.json](https://github.com/mayig/indus-valley-script-corpus/blob/main/features/P122.json)). Eleven spot checks of well-known signs pass, including P324 to M342 (jar) and P050 to M059 (fish) ([P324.json](https://github.com/mayig/indus-valley-script-corpus/blob/main/features/P324.json)). Of the nine unmapped sign types in use, P000 is a damage marker that belongs with Mahadevan's sign 0, leaving eight genuinely unmapped. A workable tie-break for the one-to-many cases is to pick the candidate with the highest count in the IM77 frequency table. The table remains **a careful working hypothesis, not a scholarly concordance**, and 58 Mahadevan numbers are never referenced in it.

The only other machine-readable table is `bridge_extended.json` in `paragorth/Indus-`, mapping Wells/ICIT numbers to Mahadevan numbers ([file](https://github.com/paragorth/Indus-/blob/HEAD/data/derived/bridge_extended.json)). The repo describes it as 143 signs; the file has 145 keys, three of them empty. It agrees with mayig on 125 of 138 comparable entries, including identical multi-value groups, which makes it very probably derived from mayig and not independent confirmation. The 13 disagreements are the places to look for errors.

The authoritative concordance exists only on paper. Fuls and Wells, "A Catalog of Indus Signs" (2023), includes concordances to both Mahadevan's and Parpola's lists ([ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/373522673_A_Catalog_of_Indus_Signs)); the catalogue itself was not opened, so its completeness is unverified. The ICIT database answers HTTP 401 and issues accounts on request ([indus.epigraphica.de](https://www.indus.epigraphica.de/)). Neither `hellosindh/indus-script-synthetic` (its own `T###` IDs over 715 signs) nor the AI-EPIGRAPHY toolkit uses Mahadevan numbers or ships a mapping ([sign_index.json](https://huggingface.co/datasets/hellosindh/indus-script-synthetic/blob/main/reference/sign_index.json), [indiahci2025](https://github.com/atulsharma0071/indiahci2025)).

## Two independent exports deliver all 2,906 IM77 texts

indusscript.in, the Roja Muthiah Research Library's web application for Mahadevan's corpus, is a Flutter app backed by Firestore with no static listing, no bulk download and per-query PDF export only ([indusscript.in](https://indusscript.in/), [app bundle](https://indusscript.in/main.dart.js)). The notes describe its access differently: the app bundle says "No signup is required for the application", with optional Google login, while both third-party exporters say they pulled the data from a signed-in browser session. The safe reading is that browsing needs no account but the bulk data was reached through an authenticated session.

Scraping it again is unnecessary. `paragorth/Indus-` ships `data/im77/im77_corpus_lines.csv` with **3,916 rows, 2,906 distinct text numbers and 14,153 tokens, of which 781 are the illegible marker `0` and 13,372 are real signs across all 417 sign numbers** ([CSV](https://github.com/paragorth/Indus-/blob/claude/indus-script-dictionary-u3dzf8/data/im77/im77_corpus_lines.csv)). These totals match Mahadevan 1977's 417 signs in 3,573 lines of 2,906 texts ([Yadav et al. 2010](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0009506)); the other 343 rows are placeholders for objects with a field symbol and no text. Each row carries site, object type, side, line, direction, field-symbol code, and the sequence in reading order with `*` marking doubtful readings ([IM77_README](https://github.com/paragorth/Indus-/blob/claude/indus-script-dictionary-u3dzf8/data/im77/IM77_README.md)). The repo's default branch is a week-old working branch, so pin the download to commit `ed56b9dc293f26a4e6bddea36c9a394e6b211ff4` and check SHA-256 `385b82bd6a9629e0ab215af46db84ca0c9ec4f6044a2a2ea58a3c64121f26211`.

Three traps in that file are worth knowing. The README says 144 readings are doubtful, but the CSV contains 504 starred signs, and the second export independently reports 504; use 504 ([converter script](https://github.com/joyboseroy/indus_decipher/blob/main/data/convert_m77_indusscript_to_csv.py)). The 155 rows whose direction is coded "no text" all contain signs, so empty rows must be filtered on `n_signs == 0`. And sequences are stored opener-first regardless of physical direction, so rendering a right-to-left impression means placing the first sign at the right edge.

The evidence for fidelity is agreement, not provenance. `joyboseroy/indus_decipher` exported the same site separately, with almost no metadata, and **all 2,906 texts match the paragorth file exactly** ([CSV](https://github.com/joyboseroy/indus_decipher/blob/main/data/m77_indusscript_real_corpus.csv)). Applying the published EBUDS filter (drop damaged, doubtful and multi-line texts, then deduplicate) to the paragorth file yields exactly 1,548 texts, 7,000 tokens and 377 signs, the figures reported for EBUDS ([Yadav et al. 2010](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0009506), [Rao et al. 2009](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.0906237106)). That gives the project a free regression test for any n-gram code. Both repos are weeks old, single-author and unstarred, and neither export was spot-checked against the printed 1977 concordance.

The alternatives do not substitute. The Hugging Face `hellosindh` set is 5,000 synthetic sequences in a non-Mahadevan numbering, stored in the opposite order, with the real training inscriptions withheld; its actual files are `synthetic_indus_5k.csv` and `reference/sign_index.json`, not the `.jsonl` its card lists ([dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/hellosindh/indus-script-synthetic)). AI-EPIGRAPHY bundles a 44-row table of letter-coded readings ([CSV](https://github.com/atulsharma0071/indiahci2025/blob/main/indus%20-%20symbols.csv)). `yajnadevam/indus-website` is downloadable under GPL-3.0 but uses ICIT-style glyph IDs ([repo](https://github.com/yajnadevam/indus-website)). EBUDS itself was never published as a file. The frequency table `im77_sign_frequencies.csv` confirms the expected skew: sign 342 (jar) has 1,395 tokens, 10.4% of the corpus, followed by 99, 59 and 267, and 112 signs occur exactly once ([frequencies](https://github.com/paragorth/Indus-/blob/claude/indus-script-dictionary-u3dzf8/data/im77/im77_sign_frequencies.csv)).

The recurring pattern is an open licence granted by an uploader over material whose upstream rights are not shown. The MIT, Apache and CC0 labels on mayig, IM-417-150 and DM-BiCLab are grants over the uploaders' own contributions; none states permission from the CISI publishers, the Archaeological Survey of India or the photographers. That matters least for mayig, which is sign codes and not photographs, and most for the DM-BiCLab sample images, whose third-party origin the paper itself states. The IM77 exports sit in the same position: joyboseroy's own registry classifies the source as "authenticated, not public bulk" ([registry](https://github.com/joyboseroy/indus_decipher/blob/main/CORPUS_REGISTRY.md)). The site's bundle says RMRL publishes the corpus "for use by researchers, students and interested members of the general public" and supplies a citation string ([app bundle](https://indusscript.in/main.dart.js)), which describes use of the app and not republication. Asking RMRL directly is the clean route.

On scraping, neither site states a prohibition. indusscript.in has no robots.txt at all, since that path returns the app shell ([robots.txt](https://indusscript.in/robots.txt)). Harappa.com's archived robots.txt sets a 10-second crawl delay and disallows only admin and search paths ([archived robots.txt](https://web.archive.org/web/2026id_/https://www.harappa.com/robots.txt)), though the site refuses automated clients in practice. Absence of a prohibition is not permission to republish, and Indian fair dealing for "private or personal use, including research" covers the student's own use, not redistribution ([s. 52 summary](https://www.mondaq.com/india/copyright/1184954/exception-to-infringement-of-copyright-section-52-of-the-copyright-act-1957)).

No source's terms address annotations made over its photographs. The precedent is consistent, though: mayig published a sign-by-sign transcription of CISI under MIT without the photographs, and DM-BiCLab and tpsatish95 released code while keeping the images out. Box coordinates, sign labels and a source identifier contain none of the photograph and follow that pattern; image crops and thumbnails do not. Trained weights are less settled, and no source speaks to them.

## Known sequences turn annotation into drawing boxes in order

The benchmark the project needs is small and buildable. Annotating 100 to 150 real photographs is the cheapest route, and the work shrinks sharply when each photo's sign sequence is already known, because the annotator draws N boxes in a known order instead of identifying each sign against a 417-entry list. The CISI plates are the only bulk source that makes this possible, since each photograph carries its CISI identifier.

This is where the concordance earns its place in Milestone 1. The IM77 exports are keyed by Mahadevan text number (1001 to 9905), not by CISI number, so they do not join to a CISI photograph directly. For Mohenjo-daro seals M-1 to about M-184, mayig's 179 transcriptions are keyed by CISI number, and its `mahadevan_graphemes` field converts them to Mahadevan IDs, with about a fifth of tokens needing the frequency tie-break or a manual check against the photo. Two other leads for a CISI-to-Mahadevan-text-number link exist and were not opened: the object-identifier crosswalk in joyboseroy's repo and the `cisi_number` column in its yajnadevam-derived file ([registry](https://github.com/joyboseroy/indus_decipher/blob/main/CORPUS_REGISTRY.md)). For synthetic training data the IM77 file is the better sequence source: sampling whole real lines preserves length, frequency and the strong positional structure that unigram sampling would destroy.

Two unknowns could quietly damage results. The class-index convention of the Kaggle IM-417-150 glyph set (1 to 417 or 0 to 416) was not checked; class 342 should be the jar and class 59 the plain fish, and an off-by-one would corrupt every label silently. The unauthenticated sign drawings at `https://indusscript.in/assets/images/<n>.png` offer a second reference for that check ([example](https://indusscript.in/assets/images/342.png)). And whether the Dixit et al. authors answer data requests is unknown, so their 245 images are a possible supplement and not a plan.

## Conclusion

The project's bottleneck has moved from finding data to making it. A month ago the sequence gap looked like a scraping problem and the concordance gap like a library trip; both are now a pinned download and a JSON field. What none of the published pipelines checked has released is ground truth for where the signs are on a real seal. A student who annotates even 100 photographs and publishes the boxes keyed to CISI numbers, without the images, would hold the first known public detection benchmark for the script, and should say plainly in the write-up that all real-image metrics are measured on self-annotated data.

The licensing picture also suggests a repo design more than a restriction. Code and annotations under MIT, a `DATA_LICENSES.md` naming each bundled item's own terms, and a fetch script with pinned commits and checksums for everything else keeps the repo reproducible without republishing anyone's photographs or corpus. Two emails would improve on that: one to RMRL asking for explicit permission to redistribute the IM77 sequences, and one to the Dixit et al. authors for their annotated images.
